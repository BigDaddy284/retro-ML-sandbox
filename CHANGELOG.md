### Added
- T-1072: a search tries first the change that got past the place before. In Boss's log of 27 Nov a search in go 129 found the way past a place on its third try, a jump of four steps at the last step its list could place a change at; the three goes after came to the same place with the same moves behind them and made 77, 75 and 63 tries there for nothing. The game, asked anew, had put the step the loss was settled at one step earlier, and a list that goes two steps at a time from its last step never came to that jump again. Nothing was kept of a way found. The runner now keeps, for each place a search found a way past (as near as half a screen, at most 32 places), the change that did it, counted from the loss it was made before (`Known`: move, hold, steps before the loss, and the plain move where it was made to the plain way), and the next search about there is told it (`find_way(known=...)`) and plays it before its lists: a change to what the AI did before anything else, a change to the plain way as soon as the plain way has lost the life. Placed by the loss, not by the step the loss was settled at. Where it gets past again it is the way (`Search.again`; the log line ends "That is the change that got past about here before, tried first."); where it does not, the search is the search it was. Measured in one and the same run on the practice game (every search made both ways from the same save, 207 searches): no way lost and none gained, 10% fewer tries. Ways of two changes are not kept, and nothing is kept from one sitting to the next. Before it, an idea that was measured and set aside: waiting for a danger that moves (see the tracker). Not tried on Boss's game.
- T-1071: what the AI remembers of a go ends before the mistake, not only before the step the game counted the life at. A life that goes for good ends the go's story `LEAD` (30) steps before it, and the way the go began with if the life went that close behind the way's end; "it" was the step of the count, and a game that takes 54 steps over a lost life counts it long after the mistake. So the story could end in the middle of the dying, and a way that ended after the mistake and more than 30 steps before the count was left as it was: in Boss's log of 27 Nov four goes in a row played the same 2,567 remembered steps into the same loss. Where the game has shown how long it takes over a loss (`dying_steps`, T-1033) that is now added to the lead, also under T-1029's doubling and for a life that goes while the way is played, and the log says so once in a game. With nothing known of the pause all is as before. On the practice bench with a long pause (four levels, 30 goes): 359 levels finished against 331, and 184 lives lost in play against 197; three levels better and one worse.
- T-1070: a search made where the go stands still no longer takes one of the go's searches after a life lost. Both kinds took from one count (three a go in the app), and Boss's log of 25 Nov has a go that searched three standstills for nothing and then lost three lives with no search made for any of them. A go may now search as many standstills as lives lost, counted apart, as the looks ahead have been since T-1052. A standstill is still searched only while the go has a search left for a life lost: three existing tests hold that (with none left the go saves no moments to go back to), and the first design, which broke them, was changed instead of them. What a go's line in the log counts is unchanged. A go can now spend the time of up to three more searches. One existing test is changed, with Boss's yes of 27 Nov for that test: `test_lives_lost_to_a_walker_are_put_down_to_it` said its practice run has at least one go that ends in the middle of losing a life; the run had two, has none now that the goes keep their searches for the lives, and the test now takes none too.
- T-1069: BizHawk is no longer left at the app's speed. "Practise fast" turns BizHawk's speed up to as much as 6400%, and the bridge script put 100% back only when the app left. Closed first, BizHawk kept that speed in its settings and started with it the next time, with the app not running (Boss's recording of 25 Nov). The script now puts 100% back as it ends, which also covers a script stopped in the Lua console while the emulator runs on. BizHawk saves its settings before it ends its scripts (read in its source), so that alone is too late for a BizHawk that is closing: the script also keeps a small note in the temp folder, `retro_ai_lab_speed_<port>.txt`, for as long as the speed is the app's, and a script that finds the note as it starts puts 100% back, removes the note and says so in the Lua console. A speed the app never set is never touched. The scripts' version stays 3. Not tried on a real BizHawk.
- T-1068: the save states the app asks BizHawk for are kept in the app's own folder. Boss's log of 25 Nov, the first sitting in five days: "A way is written down for this game from before, but it is not taken up: the saved spot it starts from could not be loaded and read (No save state in slot 'way_3c1868dd5685'.)". The bridge script wrote every save state to the temp folder, which Windows empties; since T-1064 one of them has to last until the next sitting. `ConnectionService` now tells every adapter it creates where save states are kept (`<data dir>/states`, beside `brains` and `ways`), BizHawk's adapter makes the folder and sends it with every save and load, and the script saves and loads there. A state saved by an older build is still found in the temp folder where the folder has none. The scripts and the protocol go to version 3, so that a BizHawk still running the old build's script is refused with the name of the script to open (T-891's message), where the fix would otherwise silently not be there. mGBA's script holds save states in memory and is unchanged but for its version.
- T-1067: the way written down is no longer lost before the first Start. T-1065 takes the way up at the first Start after the game is connected, but until then the way is on disk only, and every writing of the brain took the file for stale and removed it: the link going (the emulator closed first, the Disconnect button), a learning setting or the length of a go changed, another learner chosen, a saved AI loaded, the child playing first. The README had to say "press Start before you close the app again or change a setting". Now the file is left alone until a Start has had its say: it has taken the way up, or it has saved its own spot over the one the way starts from (a pinned spot, the child handing over, a way that cannot be played), which removes the file at that Start as before. A link that drops while that Start is looking for the spot leaves the chance to the first Start on the next link. Starting the AI from scratch still removes the way at once. One existing test is changed in its last assertion, with Boss's yes of 20 Nov for that test: `test_the_way_is_on_disk_when_the_link_goes` said the file goes at the first writing of the brain on a new link, and now says it stays.
- T-1066: the game's pause over a lost life is no longer taken for a dead screen. Boss's logs of 20 Nov: in every go the first life goes at the same pit, and that life got no search, where the second and third each got theirs. The game stands still for some 50 steps before it counts a life lost to a fall; nothing the learner sees changes in that time, so the go took it for stuck, looked for a way on from the middle of the pause ("Stayed about one spot for 44 steps... none got on", one of the go's three searches) and pressed the wake button, which is a step that cannot be played again and so forgets every saved moment. Where nothing has changed for as long as counts as stuck and a search could follow a life lost, the runner now first plays on off the record (`EpisodeRunner._life_going`): the plain move held for 40 steps and the dying pause it remembers (80 where it remembers none), and where that loses a life, another move held up to that step. Lost on the same step, it is the game's pause: no search where it stands, no wake press and no end as "stuck" until the life is counted (and for `LATE`, 4, steps past the step it was seen to go on), and the search after a life lost follows. Otherwise the standstill is handled on the step it was before. On the practice hopper with a pause of 200 frames a go that walks off the first edge used to end "stuck" in the pause, or press the wake button four times and lose the life with no search; it now waits, loses the life on the step it goes, goes back to before the edge and is across with every life. Not asked where the go has only stayed about one spot while what it sees keeps changing: an existing test fixes what the search there loads.
- T-1065: the way the AI remembers is taken up again when the app is opened. Boss's logs of 20 Nov showed 3,900 steps remembered at the end of one sitting and none at the beginning of the next. At the first Start after a game is connected, with no pinned spot, the app now loads the spot kept for the game (T-1064), makes sure by the game's memory that it is the one the way was played from, puts the checkpoint there and begins the first go with the way: on the practice hopper a second sitting is across the gap that cost the first one a life before the learner is asked for anything. Where it cannot be sure (the save state is gone or is another, the file is of another game, format or set of moves, or is damaged), the game is put back where it was, the log says why by name, and Start does what it always did. A pinned spot decides for itself, and later Starts in the same sitting are as before.
- T-1064: the way the AI remembers is written down. Closing the app lost the way (Boss's logs of 20 Nov: 3,900 steps gone, and an hour and a half spent getting half of them back), because it was in the app's memory only and all that said where it starts was a count of saves and a save state in a slot every game shares. The app now keeps the spot the goes start from in a save-state slot of the game's own, with a digest of the game's memory to tell it by, and writes the way in a file of its own beside the brain (a folder `ways`) whenever it writes the brain. A way that is forgotten is taken off the disk. Nothing reads the file yet, so the AI does what it did; picking the way up when the app opens is next.
- T-1063: a pinned spot keeps the way the AI remembers when you stop and start. Boss's logs of 20 Nov showed the way the AI remembered (3,900 steps, two levels) gone after the app was closed. The way belongs to the spot the goes start from, and every Start saved that spot again, which made it a new spot to everything worked out from the old one: on the practice hopper a way of 77 steps was forgotten at every Start, with a pinned spot as without one. Where the saved spot already is the pinned one, a Start now leaves it as it is and puts the game back to it, and the next go begins with the way. With no pinned spot nothing changes: Start takes the spot the game is at. This is the first of two steps; saving the way with the brain, so that it is still there when the app is opened again, is the second.
- T-1062: a way past, after the game has put the AI back. Boss's logs of 19 Nov showed every go ending the same way: the first life got to the hard spot, and the second and third went near where the game put the AI back, at things it had got past earlier in the same go. A search that tries other moves from just before a loss counted a try as "past" only where it reached ground the go had not been on, and after a put-back the go has been on all of it: on the practice hopper such a search made all its 120 tries and found none, where the same search had found the way on try 57 a life earlier. The AI now keeps, beside how far the go has got, how far the life it is on has got, started over whenever it sees that the game put it back, and a try is past where it gets somewhere this life has not been. The second life is then taken past on the very try the first was. A try from a moment before the life went counts by the life that went. What is paid does not change: ground walked again pays nothing. The log says once a session "A life was lost and the game put the AI back". Not mended here: a moment saved in the very step of a put-back still carries the place from before it, which pays walked ground again on the practice hopper (the next task).
- T-1061: the screen moves under a status bar. A stand-in driving game at the size of a real screen (outside the app) showed the AI finding its car, losing no life, and crawling: a solid status bar stands still while the road slides under it, the bar's edge outvotes the few marks on the road, and the camera was read as standing still at every look, so nothing paid for driving on. Where the whole screen says "no move" though much changed, the AI now looks for the part of the screen that moved inside strips that stood still (along the top, the bottom or a side) and reads the slide from that part, with every possible slide tried on the pixels that changed. It takes that reading only where the part that moved is the larger part of the screen, covers a quarter of it, brought something in as it took something away, explains most of what changed, and only on a game it has come to read that way; the log says so once ("A strip of the screen stands still while the rest slides"). On the stand-in the AI now drives further every go. Where the whole screen tells, nothing is different, and the practice games give the same reports. Still read in part or not at all: a slanting slide with little of it across the bar, marks that repeat, several things moving by themselves over thin scenery.

- T-1058: on a screen that does not scroll, keeping the life counts as getting past. After a life lost the AI goes back to a saved moment and tries small changes to what it did, and it took a change for the way only if it kept the life and reached ground the go had not been on (standing still in front of a gap keeps the life too). A game of one screen has no new ground once its floor is walked, so no search there could succeed: "Went back 33 steps and made 120 tries: none got past", search after search, where a step aside was all it took. - T-1057: "is the game listening" is not fooled by buttons that undo each other. Before a go the AI holds each of its buttons in turn and looks at where the game's memory ends up; in a game where left then right puts the player back exactly where it stood (a catcher, a bat, anything that walks at one speed on one screen) nothing ended up different, so the app said "The game is not listening to the AI", tried every button to wake a game that was awake, and let the game run on meanwhile with nobody playing, so goes began with a life already gone. Where the buttons together change nothing, each button is now asked by itself (held, then let go, against a run holding nothing) before the app decides. A game whose buttons together already change something is asked exactly as before, frame for frame; a screen that really ignores the pad is still found out. On a one-screen catching game kept outside the app for measuring (the simple table, 10 goes of 300 steps, two seeds): "not listening" was said three times a run and is no longer said; 10 and 7 of the 10 goes began a life down and none does now; lives lost a go 2.80 and 2.50 before, 0.90 and 2.20 after; points a go 12 and 16 before, 26 and 20 after.

- T-1055: a new way of learning that watches the screen. "How the AI learns" has a fourth choice, "Screen network": instead of a handful of numbers found in the game's memory it is fed the screen itself, shrunk to a small grey picture (32 by 28 whatever the console), what changed in it since the step before, and the buttons it last pressed; it learns from the same rewards as the others and nothing in it knows any game. Changing to it or away from it starts the AI afresh for that game (pictures and memory numbers cannot be told in each other's words), and the app asks first when there is something to lose. "Choose for me" does not pick it yet. Measured with the learning bench, every helper off: on the practice racer it learns by itself where the table and the first network did not; on the practice hopper it does not yet. Three old tests that list the ways of learning now list this one too (approved).
- T-1054: a standing measure of what the AI learns by itself. `python -m retro_ai_lab.tools.learning_bench racer` (or `hopper`) trains with every helper off (no going back, no way remembered, nothing looked ahead) and prints, for every 25 goes, the lives lost a go, how far it got and the goes with no life lost, by the game's own account; `--helpers` trains as the app does, to compare. The first figures say the learner alone learns neither practice game yet (it drives further on the racer and still loses over two of its three lives a go; on the hopper it stays near the start), which is what the next tasks are held to.
- T-1053: the last life costs points and no longer pays them. On a game that counts its lives in reserve (Boss's does) the count leaves 0 for 255 when the last one goes. The AI read that as 255 lives gained: the suggested "Gaining Lives" reward paid +5 for it, "Losing Lives" paid nothing, and a person's own "goes down" row paid nothing, so every go that ended by game over ended on pay for dying. Now a value seen to fall by one that goes from nought to the most its layout can hold (255 in a byte, 65,535 in two, 99 in a packed decimal byte) is told as fallen below nought: a life lost for the suggested rows, "went down" for a person's own row of any kind but health. The runner, which had been adding that life itself, counts it once however it is told, and a penalty paid for it is not taken for a rise that was paid for. On the practice racer made to count as his game does, a go ended by game over is now paid "Losing Lives" four times for its four lives and "Gaining Lives" not at all (three times and once before). Left as they were: health, a value that only switches between 0 and 255, a count that starts at nought and never fell by one, and how the detector calls the game started over.
- T-1052: the AI keeps away from what has cost it a life (Boss: "oh it killed me when it touch me, best keep the fuck away from them in the future"). Knowing which kinds of thing had cost a life changed nothing in what the AI did: the next one of the kind cost a life again, and only then did it go back and look for a way past. Now, when a thing of a kind that has cost it a life comes within 128 pixels across, the AI saves the game, plays what it is about to do for the next 40 steps and the game's dying pause off the record, and puts the game back. Where that would cost a life it goes back to a saved moment and looks for the way past before the life is lost, and plays it; alive, it carries on as it would have. These searches are counted apart from a go's three after a life lost; one that finds a way costs nothing, three for nothing end the looking ahead for that go, and eight in a row for nothing end it for the session. On the practice game (levels 1 to 4, both brains, both dying pauses, 12 goes each, against the version before): **57 lives lost to walkers where there were 152, 255 levels finished where there were 182**; falls the same (270 and 268: a pit is not a thing); the runs 9% longer in all and a third longer at the most. Tried first and dropped: giving the brain the lesson at the step of the touch (no change), and holding the checked move for 40 steps after a look ahead (one run then finished no level in twelve goes: the moves lined up kept it from looking for a way on at a wall). The log says "Something of a kind that has cost a life (thing-5) came near at step 131, and carrying on would cost one 88 steps on. Went back 11 steps and found a way past on try 3 ...", each go's line says "saw a life going 3 times before it went and found a way past 2 times", and the first go of a session says that the AI looks ahead. - T-1051: the AI learns what cost it a life by how the thing was met and by who was closest (Boss: "it should go oh whats that tag it, oh it killed me when it touch me ... - T-1050: a go ends when the game is over (Boss: "when it got game over the 'goes' number didn't go up"). In his log one go ran 17 minutes through 64 lives lost. His game counts the lives in reserve and takes the count below nought when the last one goes (0 to 255), which read as a life given; when a new game began the count went from 255 to 2, which read as a life lost and so never as the game starting over; and a reward of his own that pays on nearly every step kept the wait for "nothing paid" from running out. The AI now reads the game being over from the values lives lost are counted on, health guesses left out: a count that has fallen by one at least once in the go, and then goes from nought to more than it began the go with (the last life, now counted as lost) or comes back to exactly what it began with by a jump (the game gave its lives anew), ends the go, after a search for a way past where one may still be made. The goes table says "the game was over" and the go's line in the log says what the count did. Any other jump rules that value out for the rest of the go. - T-1049: ground walked again after a life lost is no longer paid twice where the screen cannot show the game putting the AI back. On plain ground the start of a level and the spot where the life went can look alike: nothing jumps, the picture reads a short slide, and the AI's count of how far it had got carried on from where it died, so every step walked again paid as new (178 steps of ground paid on a practice level that has 144).
