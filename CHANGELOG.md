# Changelog

All notable changes are recorded here, one entry per completed task. Format loosely follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/).

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- T-970: the app can find things a person tagged on the screen (an enemy, ground, a platform, something that hurts, something to pick up, the player) in later frames, facing either way and in front of any background, in about a millisecond a tag; the base for the AI seeing its surroundings (`docs/VISION.md`). Airtest was assessed and not added (ADR-011).
- T-967: a death is the value your rewards penalise most heavily (a life count or health value you penalise first, however lightly), not every penalised value, and its drops are counted as deaths whether or not the AI takes it for a life count; before, "x position goes down" counted as a death and your "Lifes" drops did not count at all.
- T-966: the survival line also gives the points per go and names the reward that moved most against the ten goes before, so a survival bought by hanging back shows up as the trade it is.
- T-965: the check of a start spot that loses a life by itself now runs the same steps holding each of the AI's buttons in turn, so a loss a button avoids (a player who stands still getting hit) is left for the AI to learn around and only a loss that comes whatever is held moves the goes' start; the start moves at most three times from one checkpoint.
- T-964: when your own reward penalises a value ("Lifes goes down", below zero), the goes count their deaths on that value, and the check of a start spot that loses a life by itself reads it too; the AI's best life-count and health guesses are the fallback, and the log names what is counted whenever that changes. The user's log had "lost a life 5 times" beside their own reward firing twice: the count was following a guess of the AI's.
- T-963: the AI learns each step from the rewards of the next eight steps added up (discounted) and the situation after them, instead of one step at a time, so a penalty reaches the move that caused it in a fifth of the passes (n-step returns; a reset or the end of a go settles the pending steps).
- T-962: the future counts 0.99 a step instead of 0.95 (a horizon of about seven seconds at four frames a step), so a life lost seconds after a mistake still reaches the mistake; a brain saved with the old setting is moved on when it is picked up, with a log line, and any other chosen value is kept.
- T-961: when three goes in a row lose their first life at the same early step, the app checks from the checkpoint, holding nothing, whether the game loses it by itself; when it does, the goes restart from just after the loss, once the game listens to the AI again. The user's log had 177 of 179 goes lose a life at step 32 whatever the AI pressed: the checkpoint was a dying moment, and every go opened with a −10 nobody could avoid.
- T-960: the emulator link's log line says what the BizHawk script's memory reads and its whole serve of a step cost (`step:reads`, `step:serve`) beside the frames, so a slow step on a user's PC names its slow part.
- T-959: the brain's learning step no longer works every situation's feature row out part by part; the feature map keeps a finished row per situation while its slots stay put, so the 65 rows a training step needs are mostly read back (a learning step at the user's scale: 3.0 ms → 1.1 ms here).
- T-958: the memory diff the tracker makes on every step no longer builds an object per changed byte up front; the objects are built the first time something reads them, which nothing on the AI's step does (the step's largest Python loop at the user's scale, for nobody).
- T-957: the AI's review no longer scans every id to name a new guess, nor builds each byte's statistics one numpy call at a time; both walks were a half of a review at the user's scale.
- T-956: the once-a-minute save takes its snapshot on the AI's thread and writes the 27 MB file on a worker thread, in pieces the interpreter can switch away from, so the AI no longer stands still for the 1.2 s the write takes; a stop, a disconnect and the Save button still write at once.
- T-955: the log says how big the knowledge is, once when a game's knowledge is picked up and once per save, with the time the save took.
- T-954: the Research window refreshes on a finished go or a state change only while it is open; closed, it waits to be opened, which already refreshes it, instead of rebuilding its tables at every go's end for nobody.
- T-953: a guess is found by its address through an index the knowledge keeps, instead of a walk over every guess; the Research window's memory table asked that question 1,800 times at every go's end.
- T-952: the knowledge keeps its confident relationships in an index that every write keeps up to date, so the panel's "What leads to what" list, the reward suggestions and the goals no longer walk every relationship and recompute its confidence to find the few that matter (the panel refresh took 136 ms a time on the GUI thread in the user's log).
- T-951: the AI's review proposes new guesses by looking each candidate's bytes up in a set of the bytes its variables already span, instead of comparing every candidate with every variable (about a million comparisons a review at the user's scale, the review's largest share); the same guesses are added as before.
- T-950: the big-region scan test takes the best of three runs and allows 50 ms or a plain Python pass over the same bytes, whichever is longer, after tripping twice on slow CI runners while the scan was fine.
- T-949: the AI's own changes to the knowledge are saved at most once a minute while it plays instead of after every review and changed guess; a stop, a disconnect and the Save button still write at once. The user's stage log showed a 1.1 s save every 9 s, each one holding the AI's step.
- T-948: the suggestions no longer walk every relationship twice a step to see whether a reward or goal suggestion should change; the knowledge counts changes to its confident relationships in `confident_relationships_version` and the watch compares that number. The user's stage log put the walk at 56 ms of a 105 ms step.
- T-947: the "App work" log line times the AI's step stage by stage (`step: choose`, `step: emulator`, `step: observe`, `step: encode`, `step: learn`) and the discovery service's share of it (`observe: track`, `discovery: events`, `long look`, `relationships`, `announce`, `review`, `experiment`, `save`), so a slow step on a user's PC names its slow stage; the user's log showed 120 ms steps with the emulator at 19 ms and no line saying where the rest went.
- T-946: the relationship finder writes a pair at once only when its support grows (each once, with the frame's final counts), and the pairs whose cause or effect merely happened again at a flush every 15 steps and before a save, instead of rebuilding every stored pair on every call; a synthetic step at the user's scale spends about 2.8 ms on it instead of 6.5. A rate in the Discovery panel can be up to 15 steps old; a saved knowledge is exact.
- T-945: the change tracker counts every byte of a region in numpy arrays instead of a Python loop (the same statistics, roll-overs, carries and restores included, checked byte for byte against the old loop by a property test), and the diff finds changed bytes in one comparison; a synthetic step at the user's scale spends about 1 ms on it instead of 5. A `ByteStats` reading is now a fresh object built from the arrays, not a live one.
- T-944: "Practise fast" rests the panels: the Discovery and Rewards panels refresh once every 2 s instead of several times a second while the AI practises fast, and catch up on pause, stop or "Watch it play"; `TrainingService.practising_fast` names the state (the Game panel's picture rate follows it too) and `Coalescer.rest` the pause. The user's recording of fast practice ran at two thirds of normal speed: the emulator took 30 ms a step and the app 140 ms, with the window's thread busy more than half the time refreshing the knowledge panel after every step.
- T-943: a reward row a person removes stays removed for that game: the profile keeps the removed rules (a kind of event on a kind of value, the Starting over row, a button row) and the suggestions, the reconnect and the AI's next guess of that kind leave them out; Remove works on every row without telling the AI a guess is wrong, and "Bring back suggestions" undoes the removals; the user found removed rows "auto readded" and could not remove most of the table.
- T-942: while a person plays, a BizHawk link runs the game at the emulator's own pace: Play switches it to free run, every pulse watches one frame (reads, pad, picture) instead of sending the frames the wall clock owes in bursts, Pause holds it again, the script leaves the real controller alone between watched steps, and training holds the game before the AI's first step. The user found play "difficult with all the pause buffers going on".
- T-941: a number a game holds over several bytes is one value: the knowledge gains sized layouts (binary, packed decimal and one digit per byte, either order) with a length per value; Search values keeps candidate values instead of bytes, extends a found number over its leading zeros, follows the decoded number on every scan and shows one row per value with how it is held; naming or rewarding one makes a value of that layout and sets the AI's byte guesses inside it aside; the Rewards table and the discovery list show a value's byte range. The user tracked the score's hundreds digit, which went back to 0 at 1,000.
- T-940: the go line's "lost a life N times" and the survival line count only the setbacks of the knowledge's best life count and best health (the person's confirmed one first), set by the runner at every go, instead of every guess of those kinds; the user's 950-go log said 45 lives lost a go while the confirmed Lifes row paid about once.
- T-939: a game has one life count, one health and one score: when a person confirms or names a guess of one of those kinds, the AI's own other guesses of that kind are set aside by the person's word (not rethought), so they leave the rewards, the steps, what the AI sees and the survival count at once; the user's 950-go log counted "45 lives lost" a go from three timer-like life counts beside the confirmed one.
- T-938: a suggested step whose guess the AI no longer has at all is retired, as one whose guess was rejected already was, so the Progress panel stops piling up "Get further in Progress" steps "detected by Progress of v12" from guesses that are gone; while the knowledge is empty nothing is retired, and a person's own steps are never touched.
- T-937: the Rewards panel's Remove button works on every row: a reward of one's own is taken out as before, and a row the AI suggested tells the AI its guess is wrong, so its rewards go and the byte is not guessed again; a row with no guess behind it says to untick it instead; the user's wrong guesses looked "hard baked" because Remove only took out rewards of one's own.
- T-936: the value search takes any number the game shows, up to 99,999,999, instead of 0 to 255: `patterns(value)` lists every byte sequence a game might hold it as (binary over one to four bytes in either byte order, packed BCD in either order, one digit per byte in either order), `holding` marks every byte that takes part, and New search and Is exactly keep those bytes; the user typed the score 300 and was told a byte holds 0 to 255.
- T-935: a checkpoint, practise-spot or experiment restore is not a change: the change statistics move past the jump the next snapshot shows after a `load_state` without counting a sample, change, up, down, hop or wrap (the diff is still returned), the experiment harness tells the observation service after every restore and the training and discovery services wire it up; the user's 20:03 log said the confirmed lives byte "went up 17 times and down 17" when every up was a go starting from the checkpoint.
- T-934: survival in the log: each go's line says how many lives were lost and at which step the first went, the go result keeps both, and every tenth go from the twentieth a line compares lives lost per go and the steps before the first loss over the last ten goes with the ten before; the user's 20:03 log needed 160 go lines counted by hand to say the AI was dying more as it trained.
- T-933: the log says at every connection whether the saved brain was picked up (with its goes and steps so far), that there is none yet, or why it could not be used, and records a "Start from scratch"; the Learning panel asks before Start from scratch deletes the brain; the user's 20:03 log had five fresh brains in two hours and nothing saying why.
- T-932: exploring follows results: when the last ten goes score worse than the ten before, the chance of a random move goes back up to `Settings.rebound` (25%) and is held there while the goes stay worse, easing off over ten goes once they recover; the runner hands each go's reward to the learner, the brain saves the last goes, the Research window gets "Exploring rebound", and the rise is logged; the user's 20:03 log had exploring at its 5% floor from go 14 while lives lost per go climbed from 0.17 to 1.83.
- T-931: own positions moved by the same buttons are copies and count once in the distance pairs, so the eight distance seats go to eight moving things; the user's log had one enemy measured from four sprite columns of the player.
- T-930: a button whose trial changes at least half of what the game changes on its own in those frames (a pause button, a menu, Start on a title screen) is linked to no value and named in the experiment line; on the user's game Start pauses, so every enemy sprite had been linked to it and locked out of the moving things.
- T-929: each button experiment logs the values the buttons moved and the buttons that moved nothing, and the "It sees" line after every go names the position the distances to moving things are measured from and whether the stick moves it, so a log can show whether the AI knows its own position.
- T-928: the AI's strongest sixteen guesses of a kind count moving things apart from plain positions, so the user's sixteen climbing positions no longer push every enemy sprite out of what the brain and the distance seats can see; the Rewards panel still offers plain positions only.
- T-927: each census line also says how often a value's hop repeated the one before and which hop that was, the numbers the T-924 counter rule reads, so a log can show why a steady stepper is or is not taken for a clock.
- T-926: the "judged on N changes" record behind each guess is forgotten when a game connects, because the change statistics start from zero with every connection; the user's run on the T-924 build rethought nothing, its guesses having been judged on 60,000 changes of an earlier run that the new run's 30,000 could never double.
- T-925: the Learning panel's fast-tick test takes its five steps under a time budget of its own and checks the panel's 20 ms budget separately, so a slow Windows CI runner no longer fails it; it had failed on two pull requests and passed on re-run each time.
- T-924: a value that climbs by the same hop as its previous change nearly every step is a counter even when the game resets it now and then, so the user's frame counter, which jumped back to zero once in thousands of steps and so kept a position seat past T-921, is a counter at last; the tracker now keeps each value's last hop and how often a hop repeated.
- T-923: the brain's distance seats now pair the best-believed moving things with the AI's own position whether or not those things won one of its few position seats, so dozens of enemy sprites ranked below plainer positions still reach the brain as distances; the log's "(N moving)" counts them too.
- T-922: the AI also rethinks a guess when it keeps the kind but for other reasons, so a position first seen climbing becomes "Something moving" once it has moved both ways; the user's enemy sprites had stayed plain positions, invisible to the brain's distance seats, for that reason.
- T-921: a value that climbs by the same amount nearly every step, the way a frame counter does when read every four frames, is a counter and no longer takes a position seat; the user's census showed one sitting among the AI's six positions.
- T-920: the census now has three lists, the busiest values, the smoothest (small hops in three changes of four) and the widest-ranging rare ones, so a thing on screen for seconds of each go is listed even though random values change every step; the user's first census had every line taken by such values.
- T-919: every 25th review the log prints a census of the sixteen busiest memory values (how often each changes, how many values it has taken, its small, unit and largest hops, ups, downs and roll-overs, and what the AI calls it), so a log can show why a value is or is not taken for something moving.
- T-918: a thing that is on screen only now and then (an enemy for a few seconds of each go) is still "Something moving": three small hops in four make a value smooth, since each visit starts and ends with one big hop, and a value that changes rarely over the whole session counts once it has moved through more than sixteen values. The user's log had hundreds of rethinks and not one moving thing because enemies were too brief to qualify.
- T-917: the AI rethinks a guess only once the value has changed at least twice as often as when it last judged it, so a value on the edge of a rule no longer flips kind at every review (the user's log had 1,384 flips in an hour, mostly progress to unknown and back); each guess remembers how many changes it was judged on.
- T-916: the AI rethinks any guess it made itself whenever a review finds its behaviour now says another kind, so an enemy's position that looked like "a speed" after five changes becomes "Something moving" once it has walked; a guess the AI dropped comes back the same way as another kind. Guesses you confirmed, named or rejected, ones placed by your "I just …" or by the restart finding, and positions the controller link found are never rethought. The user's log had run 77 goes on labels given at the first look.
- T-915: the distances to moving things are measured from the AI's own position, the one the stick moves or one you confirmed, not from a scrolling value that happened to rank higher.
- T-914: a value that changes every step by small hops, the way a walking enemy's position does, is no longer thrown away as noise; it is listed as "Something moving" whichever way it goes. The user's log showed the AI had never seen a moving thing because of this.
- T-913: after every go the log says which brain the AI is using, how many situations it knows, how often it still tries moves at random and what it sees (positions, moving things, distances, life counts, switches …), so a log can answer "why does it play the same way every go?".
- T-912: the brain now sees how far each "Something moving" is from the AI's own position, exact up close and rough far away, so a thing about to touch it and one across the screen are different situations; eight of its seats are kept for these distances whenever anything moves.
- T-911: a memory value that wanders both ways in small steps, often, is now taken for the position of something that moves (an enemy, a platform), so the brain can see what it keeps dying to; it is called "Something moving" until a button is shown to move it, and the long look does not read progress off it.
- T-910: the freeze at the end of each go is gone: the Learning panel's "what the AI does differently" reads the snapshot the AI took as the go ended instead of running the brain over every remembered situation on the window's thread.

- T-908: the brain's slots go to the useful values. The AI's state now takes its values kind by kind (positions, lives, health, speed, score, progress, then flags), the most trusted of each kind first, so a position found late is seen before the twentieth switch; and a full brain gives a newcomer the slot of a value that is absent instead of never seeing it.
